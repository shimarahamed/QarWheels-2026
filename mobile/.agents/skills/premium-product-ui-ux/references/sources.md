# Source library: standards, research, and instructional videos

**Purpose:** Consult authoritative sources when a task needs normative requirements or detailed evidence. The 160 patterns are editorial, original implementation heuristics, **not** a verbatim reproduction or endorsement by these publishers. YouTube tutorials illustrate workflows; they do not establish universal UX rules. References checked: 2026-11-03.

## Normative / platform guidance (highest priority for applicable contexts)

1. W3C, **WCAG 2.2** — https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ — accessibility requirements; respect precise criteria and exceptions. For web, AA text contrast is generally 4.5:1, with 3:1 for qualifying large text; 2.5.8 target size has exceptions.
2. W3C, **Understanding WCAG** — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/ — implementation-oriented lookup; check current conformance language.
3. Apple, **Human Interface Guidelines** — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ — native platform conventions, system controls and motion accessibility.
4. Apple, **Motion** — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion — purposeful motion, system preferences and accessibility.
5. Material Design 3 — https://m3.material.io/ — mobile/platform design patterns; check guidance for chosen platform and version.
6. GOV.UK Design System, **Components** — https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/ — form and service patterns with accessible coded examples.
7. GOV.UK Design System, **Error messages** — https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/ — actionable validation error wording and preservation of values.
8. GOV.UK Design System, **Error summaries** — https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/ — summary links, focus guidance and recovery.

## Research / measurement (evidence-informed heuristics; apply to context)

9. Nielsen Norman Group, **10 Usability Heuristics** — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ — familiar usability heuristics, not a deterministic checklist.
10. Baymard Institute, **Checkout UX** — https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux — evidence-informed checkout problems; do not extrapolate checkout-specific findings to every product.
11. Baymard Institute, **Reducing checkout fields** — https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields — forms and payment flow efficiency.
12. web.dev, **Core Web Vitals thresholds** — https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds — LCP, INP, CLS field metrics and interpretation.
13. web.dev, **Learn Accessibility** — https://web.dev/learn/accessibility — practical web implementation topics.

## Source selection discipline

- **Standards** define requirements or platform expectations within their own scope.
- **Research** informs design choices but may be population- and domain-specific.
- **Tutorials** demonstrate technique, not effectiveness across contexts.
- **Video examples** may be stylized; test their accessibility, performance and interaction honesty before production.
- Where a specific law or standard applies, confirm the current local and platform requirements rather than assuming this library is legal advice.
